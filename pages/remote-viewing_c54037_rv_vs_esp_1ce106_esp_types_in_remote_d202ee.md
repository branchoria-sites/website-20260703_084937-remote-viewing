## Introduction

[Remote viewing]({{ 'remote-viewing/' | relative_url }}) is often described as a specialised way of testing alleged extrasensory perception (ESP), but the type of ESP involved depends on how the target is chosen and how information is supposed to be obtained. A session aimed at describing a hidden location resembles **clairvoyance**; one involving a person at the target site may be interpreted as **telepathy**; and a session in which the target is selected or revealed only after the viewing can be framed as **[precognition]({{ 'precognition/' | relative_url }})**. This flexibility is one reason remote viewing occupies an unusual position within parapsychology. Rather than claiming a single psychic mechanism, many remote-viewing protocols are designed so that different interpretations remain possible depending on the experimental design. At the same time, critics argue that changing the proposed mechanism after the fact makes the claims harder to test decisively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

## Hidden Targets and Clairvoyance

The form of ESP most closely associated with remote viewing is clairvoyance. In parapsychology, clairvoyance refers to the alleged acquisition of information about an object, place or event without using the ordinary senses and without relying on another person's thoughts. Remote-viewing experiments commonly fit this description because the viewer attempts to describe a concealed target that nobody in the testing room can directly observe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

Classic laboratory protocols sought to isolate this possibility by introducing sensory shielding. A target location, photograph or object was randomly selected, the viewer was prevented from seeing it, and impressions were recorded before any feedback. If accurate descriptions occurred under these conditions, supporters argued that clairvoyance was the simplest interpretation because no known sensory pathway connected the viewer with the target. Early Stanford Research Institute (SRI) studies by Harold Puthoff and Russell Targ were explicitly presented as investigations of information acquisition under sensory shielding rather than conventional perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

However, this interpretation has remained controversial. Critics examining the original experiments argued that subtle [sensory cues]({{ 'sensory-cues/' | relative_url }}), transcript editing and procedural weaknesses could account for some reported successes. Later reviews commissioned for the CIA concluded that, although some statistical anomalies were reported in parts of the research programme, the evidence was not sufficiently reliable or operationally useful to establish clairvoyance as a demonstrated phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

## Beacon People and Telepathy

Some remote-viewing experiments introduced a "beacon" or "sender"—a person physically present at the target location while the viewer remained elsewhere. This design raises a different question: if accurate descriptions occur, is the viewer obtaining information directly from the location, or indirectly from the beacon's mind?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

Within parapsychology, this distinction matters because telepathy concerns alleged mind-to-mind communication rather than direct perception of distant places or objects. If the beacon is concentrating on distinctive features of the target, successful descriptions could be interpreted as telepathic access to the beacon's mental experience rather than clairvoyant access to the target itself. As a result, some experiments attempted to separate these possibilities by varying whether a human observer was present at the target or by designing [targets]({{ 'targets/' | relative_url }}) that no individual knew in advance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

Researchers also recognised that ordinary information [leakage]({{ 'leakage/' | relative_url }}) had to be excluded. Experimenters, monitors and judges could unintentionally transmit cues through conversation, body language or document handling. Modern [protocol]({{ 'protocol/' | relative_url }}) design therefore emphasised blind or double-blind procedures to reduce both conventional cueing and the possibility that any apparent success could simply reflect knowledge shared by participants rather than a paranormal process.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

## Future Selection and Precognition

Perhaps the most unusual interpretation arises when the target is not fixed until after the viewing session has finished. In these experiments, viewers record descriptions before a random process later determines which target will become the correct one. If correspondences exceed chance expectations, supporters argue that clairvoyance and telepathy become less satisfactory explanations because no present target yet exists to observe and no person knows the future selection. Instead, the proposed mechanism shifts towards precognition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://scispace.com/pdf/remote-viewing-the-future-with-a-tasking-temporal-outbounder-4ba1gpxihd.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scispace.com">[SciSpace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scispace.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sci Spaceremote-viewing-the-future-with-a-tasking-temporal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">remote-viewing-the-future-with-a-tasking-temporal-...Abstract—This study uses remote viewing in a predictive manner within the c...</span></span></span>

Parapsychologists have long debated whether successful remote viewing in these circumstances represents genuine foreknowledge or some broader phenomenon often labelled "psi", in which distinctions between clairvoyance, telepathy and precognition become artificial. Some researchers have suggested that feedback itself—seeing the correct target after the session—might somehow influence the earlier viewing, while others argue that such ideas require forms of retrocausality that challenge conventional understandings of time and causation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342061969_What_Do_We_Know_About_Psi_The_First_Decade_of_Remote_Viewing_Research_and_Operations_at_Stanford_Research_Institute" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) What Do We Know about Psi?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The First Decade of...December 30, 2019 — 7 days ago — Remote viewing is a nonanalytic ability; describing a distant shape, form, or loc...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 30, 2019</span></span></span>

These proposals remain highly controversial because they conflict with established physical theories and have not achieved broad acceptance within mainstream science. Independent replication under rigorous controls has also remained inconsistent, leaving precognitive interpretations unconfirmed despite continued interest within parts of the parapsychology community.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10275521/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCFollow‐up on the U.S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Central Intelligence Agency&#x27;s (CIA...by Á Escolà‐Gascón · 2023 · Cited by 10 — Since 1972, the U.S. Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) co...</span></span></span>

## Why the Categories Often Overlap

Remote-viewing researchers have frequently noted that the traditional ESP categories can blur together during experiments. A viewer describing a hidden military installation might appear to demonstrate clairvoyance if the target is already fixed. The same session could be interpreted as telepathy if another participant is observing the location. If the target is selected only after the session, identical descriptions may instead be labelled precognition.

Because these interpretations depend heavily on protocol rather than on the viewer's subjective experience, many researchers prefer the broader term "[anomalous cognition]({{ 'not-clairvoyance/' | relative_url }})" or simply "psi". The emphasis shifts away from identifying a specific psychic faculty and towards asking whether any information is obtained beyond ordinary sensory channels. This terminology was adopted in parts of the later U.S. government research programme to avoid assuming a particular mechanism before experimental evidence established one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>

## What the Debate Means for Evaluating Remote Viewing

The relationship between remote viewing and the classic ESP categories illustrates an important methodological issue rather than proving any particular psychic ability. Experimental design determines which mechanism is being tested:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Hidden current targets** primarily test claims analogous to clairvoyance.
* **Targets involving a human beacon** allow telepathy to become a possible interpretation.
* **Targets chosen after the session** are designed to test claims resembling precognition.

</div>

This flexibility makes remote viewing a more structured testing framework than many traditional psychic practices, but it also complicates interpretation. A positive result does not automatically identify which form of ESP, if any, is responsible. Conversely, negative or inconsistent findings challenge all three interpretations simultaneously. The continuing scientific debate therefore centres less on choosing between clairvoyance, telepathy and precognition than on whether any reliable evidence exists for information acquisition beyond known sensory and cognitive processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp96-00791r000200180006-4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[Wikipedia+3CIA+3PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AN EVALUATION OF REMOTE VIEWING: RESEARCH...A remote viewer is asked to visualize a place, location, or object being viewed by a &quot;bea...</span></span></span>
